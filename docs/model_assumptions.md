# Model assumptions and non-claims

This document states what the numerical model **is** and **is not**. Read it before interpreting any number in `outputs/`.

## What the model is

A reduced-order, microscale, two-phase materials study on **synthetic** FiberForm-*like* voxel geometries:

1. Carbon solid (label 1) and pore space (label 0).
2. Approximate random cylindrical fibers with controllable in-plane and out-of-plane angular spread.
3. Oxygen transport by a single effective diffusivity in the pores, plus a first-order sink at the solid–pore interface.
4. Approximate directional effective conductivity from a steady finite-difference solve on the same grid.

The machine-learning piece is a **tabular physics-guided surrogate**: Random Forest and Histogram Gradient Boosting trained on features and targets produced by those solvers. It is **not** a PINO, FNO, or 3-D neural operator.

## Voxelized synthetic geometry

- Fibers are rasterized cylinders on a Cartesian grid. Surfaces are staircased.
- There is no claim that a generated volume is a reconstruction of NASA FiberForm, PICA, or any flight billet.
- Connectivity, pore size, and orientation descriptors are **proxies**, not tomographic measurements.
- Degenerate volumes (fully solid, fully pore, or porosity far from the requested target) raise `DegenerateVolumeError` instead of being passed downstream.

## Two-phase material assumption

The solid is assigned a single isotropic conductivity `k_solid`. The pore space is assigned a single `k_pore`. Real carbon fibers are anisotropic; real pore gas composition, temperature, and rarefaction vary through a heat shield. Those effects are omitted.

## Effective diffusion approximation

Oxygen exists only in pore voxels. The model is

```
dC/dt = Deff ∇²C − k_reaction C  (at interface pores only)
```

`Deff` is an effective coefficient, not a dusty-gas or Knudsen multi-species flux. There is no Stefan flow, no CO/CO2 chemistry, and no temperature-dependent diffusivity.

## Simplified reaction kinetics

The sink is first-order in a **normalized** concentration. It is not an Arrhenius law, not a shrinking-core law, and not the Ferguson et al. (2016) oxidation model. Reaction does not consume solid in the default configuration. An optional degradation switch can convert a few highly damaged interface voxels to pore; that switch is exploratory and off by default.

## No full reentry aerochemistry

Boundary conditions are laboratory-style: C = 1 at the z = 0 pore face, zero flux on the sides, and a configurable condition at z = max. There is no incoming boundary-layer profile, no ablation blowing, no shock, and no vehicle trajectory.

## No resin pyrolysis in this baseline

PICA-like materials include phenolic resin. This repository does not model pyrolysis gas, char, or resin mass loss.

## No direct experimental validation

There are no TGA curves, no micro-CT comparisons, and no arc-jet data in this project. PuMA is an **optional** future integration, not a validation source for the numbers this pipeline reports.

## Surrogate vs. baseline vs. reality

**Agreement between the surrogate and the numerical baseline demonstrates that the surrogate learned the simplified numerical model. It does not demonstrate that the simplified model matches physical reality.**

Every report generated by `reporting.py` repeats this sentence. Do not delete it.

## Interpret results comparatively

Use the pipeline to ask questions such as:

- Does oxidation penetration increase with reaction rate at fixed microstructure (inside this model)?
- Does through-thickness `k_eff,z` decrease as porosity increases (inside this model)?
- Can a tabular surrogate reproduce those trends faster than re-solving the PDE?

Do **not** treat predicted `k_eff` or oxidation depth as flight heat-shield performance, TPS thickness requirements, or NASA-approved values. Constants in YAML files are **illustrative / configurable** unless a source is cited in `references.md`.
